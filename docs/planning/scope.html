One walk becomes a history

Two things must be demonstrated

The complete outdoor loop

Use real photos and normal app controls on the learner’s Android phone in current Chrome. Save a new cat and a repeat encounter, then close and reopen the app to verify the history remains. The interaction should be short enough to keep walking.

Controlled matching evaluation

Use roughly 8–12 known cats and 40–60 varied photos, including similar-looking cats. Fix the strategy and conservative threshold on development photos before inspecting a genuine held-out set. Indoor evaluation photos do not replace actual Android/outdoor encounters.

Missing a match is acceptable: manually choose an existing cat. Poor separation means familiar-face matching is not proven, even if inference works. This is a PoC evaluation, not a general accuracy claim. The learner explicitly expanded the dataset during technical planning.

The boundary

Open each section to review what belongs in this experiment.

Now: the smallest complete encounter loop
  • Camera/photo input; local detector, crop, embedding, and similarity.
  • Current models: onnx-community/yolov10n and Xenova/dinov2-small.
  • Human confirmation, manual existing-cat selection, and manual naming.
  • Optional note and opt-in private location.
  • Local persistence, scrapbook, counts, and encounter history.
  • First-use download/loading, no-cat and retry states, and selection when multiple cats are detected.
  • Real-device feasibility and a practical WebGPU-unavailable fallback, validated rather than assumed.
Later: useful additions that do not prove the idea

Other browsers, installable PWA/offline shell, private maps, export/import, achievements, and decorative polish.

Cut: additions that dilute or contradict the core
  • Accounts, social features, and cloud backend/inference: unnecessary for this private local loop.
  • Public exact-location maps: incompatible with private sightings.
  • Automatic identity, generated names, and fictional biographies: people own naming and identity.
  • Other animals and broader game mechanics: distract from the cat encounter proof.

Spend the time on the uncertainty first

12–16 focused hours before October 11, 2026. Android Chrome is the outdoor target; Windows Chrome is the development and profiling environment.

  1. Prove detector, crop, embedding, and similarity on the actual phone.
  2. Complete capture → confirm/name → save → scrapbook.
  3. Check mobile usability, failures, privacy, and real outdoor use.
  4. Polish only after the proof works.

If models are impractical or matching fails, revisit or simplify that part with the learner early. Do not silently replace agreed models or claim success.

What this sharpens in the existing plan

Manual existing-cat selection is required here; docs/MVP.md currently calls it optional. This draft also focuses validation on Android Chrome. Detailed latency limits, fallback implementation, and a reproducible similarity trial belong in the next planning steps.

The learning goal is practical browser-local AI and disciplined, traceable implementation. The submission target remains the Hacktoberfest DEV Touch Grass challenge, not a substituted Devpost event.
